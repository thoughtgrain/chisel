Design concept · October 2026

CHISEL

A sculpting-sampler firmware concept for the Akai MPC Sample, inspired with love for the Torso S-4. It runs four tracks, each with five devices in a chain. You play it with sixteen pressure pads and three knobs.

I own two S-4s, and if you like what this does, I recommend supporting Torso and getting the real thing. This is just my take on how those ideas could live on different hardware. It's a design spec, not shipping software. Akai announced on Oct 28, 2026 that it's opening the MPC Sample to custom firmware, and this is what I'd want to build on it.

01

The panel is the signal flow

What I love about the S-4 is that its interface is its signal chain. The device buttons sit in chain order, and the encoders map straight onto what's on screen. You never wonder where you are.

The MPC Sample doesn't have nine encoders, but it has something the S-4 lacks: sixteen pads with poly aftertouch. So instead of copying the S-4's controls, I move the navigation onto the pads and let pressure do work that buttons can't.

  1. The pad grid is the chain. You can see where you are from the pads alone, without reading the screen.
  2. Three knobs, three values. A screen never shows more than the three knobs can reach.
  3. Pressure is a mod source. The S-4's action buttons become pressure pads, so actions like Freeze or Wash can be played partway, not just switched on or off.
  4. Two presses to anything. Any parameter is a device pad plus an F-button away.

02

Two boxes, one gap

The MPC Sample has fewer continuous controls and a smaller screen than the S-4, but far more playable surface. So the design shifts work off the knobs and onto the pads.

03

Four tracks, same chain

I'm keeping the S-4's shape exactly. Each track runs Material → Granular → Filter → Color → Space, and all four sum through a mixer that has a DJ filter per channel and a compressor on the master bus. The one addition is pad pressure, a fifth mod source next to the four modulators.

04

What the pads do

CHISEL takes over five stock buttons as modes. Pressing the active mode's button again always returns to Chain, so there's one way home from anywhere. Try it: pick a mode, then tap pads. In Chain, the track and device pads change what the bottom row does.

Top row = pads 13–16 · pad 1 is bottom-left

  • Full: focused or selected
  • Ring: active
  • Dashed: bypassed, muted, empty
  • Neutral: utility
  • Light, with bar: pressure-sensitive

05

Knobs, pages and the fader

Each control has exactly one job per mode, and the screen always says what that job is. My rule: if you have to remember what a control does, the screen failed.

Knobs 1–3
Control the three values on screen, left to right. Each knob sits directly under its value.
F1–F3
Switch between the focused device's three named pages. The names print right above the buttons. Shift + F resets that knob's value.
Fader
Sets the device's Amount (dry/wet) in Chain, morphs between scenes in Perform, and sets track level in Mix.
Encoder
Turning it fine-adjusts the last-touched value in 1/10 steps. Pressing it moves to the next value. It also scrolls lists.
Shift + pad
Reaches global functions: save, project, settings, MIDI. That leaves the mode buttons for modes only.

Knob takeover, made visible

The MPC Sample's knobs are absolute pots, so they're almost never sitting where the parameter is. I default to scaled takeover, building on the modes Akai added in firmware 1.3. The value moves the way you turn, at a rate that converges on the knob's real position, so nothing jumps.

The screen also shows the gap. A hollow tick on each value's arc marks where the physical knob actually is. When the tick and the value meet, the tick disappears.

06

Every screen, same four zones

From top to bottom: a status bar, the F-button labels, a visual, and three values over the three knobs. Navigation is hub and spokes. Chain is home, every other screen is one press away, and nothing nests deeper than device → page. The mockups below are drawn at 2× an assumed 320 × 240 panel.

07

Accessible on a 2.4-inch screen

The biggest risks are legibility and meaning carried by color alone. I'm using WCAG 2.2 as the bar where it applies to hardware, and going past it where the small screen demands. These are design rules, so they cost nothing to adopt now and a lot to retrofit later.

Vision

Value numerals are at least 20 px and labels at least 11 px, with 7:1 contrast. A Large mode shows one value per screen in huge type.

Color

Track colors come from the Okabe-Ito palette, and each track also has a glyph (● ▲ ■ ◆). Pad state is shown by brightness and border style, never by hue alone.

Motor

Every hold has a latch alternative, and no gesture depends on timing. Pressure curve and pad sensitivity are adjustable.

Cognitive

Breadcrumbs are always on screen, and every mode has the same one-press way home. Holding Shift draws a legend of what each pad does.

Non-visual

The UI state could stream over USB MIDI to a companion app that reads it aloud. Optional quiet ticks confirm mode and page changes.

Photosensitivity

Pads and screen never flash more than 3 times a second. Tempo blinks are soft pulses, not hard on/off.

08

Open questions

  • What does Akai's firmware SDK expose: pad LEDs, raw poly aftertouch, button remapping, DSP access, USB MIDI out?
  • What's the screen's native resolution? Every type size here assumes 320 × 240.
  • Does the CPU have headroom for 4 granular tracks at 128 grains each, or should grains be capped per track?
  • Chain mode uses rows for functions and Mix uses columns for tracks. Is that mismatch comfortable in the hand?
  • Next step: a clickable prototype to test the two-press rule.
